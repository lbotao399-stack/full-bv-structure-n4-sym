[S2.02_Siegel1980] W. Siegel -- Inconsistency of supersymmetric dimensional regularization (1980)
  arXiv: none (pre-arXiv)   Journal: Phys. Lett. B 94 (1980) 37-40
  Section: 2 DRED consistency / FDH / HV / epsilon-scalars
  Claim: Shows that DRED as proposed in S2.01 is mathematically inconsistent and outlines what must be changed for a consistent version.
  Loop order / theory: algebraic (order-independent) statement / any theory regularized by DRED
  Scheme: DRED with 4-dimensional gamma matrices / epsilon tensors and a d-dimensional (d<4) momentum space embedded in the 4-dimensional index space.
  Symmetry argument: Not a symmetry argument: an algebraic contradiction. As reported in S2.10 (Sec. 1): combining 4-dimensional gamma matrices and epsilon tensors with a d-dimensional metric yields 0 = d(d-1)(d-2)(d-3)(d-4); the trigger is the projector relation g^{mu nu} ghat_nu^rho = ghat^{mu rho} (eq. 4 of S2.10) used together with genuinely 4-dimensional epsilon/metric identities. S2.09 (Sec. 3, eqs. 15-19) phrases it as contradictions such as (d+1)(d-2) epsilonhat^{mu nu} = 0 obtained by evaluating epsilon-epsilon contractions in different orders.
  Proved: 
      - The combination 'd-dimensional momenta inside a strictly 4-dimensional index space with all 4-dimensional identities available' is contradictory for non-integer d.
  Assumed: (none)
  Checked partially: (none)
  Evidence: 
      - INSPIRE abstract: scheme mathematically inconsistent; required modifications outlined.
      - S2.10 hep-ph/0503129 Sec. 1-2: the identity 0 = D(D-1)(D-2)(D-3)(D-4) (unnumbered), eqs. (3)-(4) of that paper identify the ingredients.
      - S2.09 hep-ph/9707278 Sec. 3, eqs. (15)-(19).
  Red flags: 
      - The contradiction implies that a consistent DRED must forbid purely 4-dimensional identities (index counting, 5-index antisymmetrisation, Fierz rearrangements, epsilon-tensor products) inside d-dimensional expressions; but the classical SUSY invariance of the action uses Fierz identities, so a consistent DRED can break SUSY (made explicit in S2.10).
