[S1a.23_EJS2004] B. Eden, C. Jarczak, E. Sokatchev -- A three-loop test of the dilatation operator in N=4 SYM (2004)
  arXiv: hep-th/0409009   Journal: not shown in the fetched text (not verified)
  Section: 1a N=4 spectrum via Feynman/supergraphs
  Claim: Three-loop anomalous dimensions of the first two BMN two-impurity multiplets (Konishi, J=0, twist two; and the J=1, twist-three multiplet) from two-point functions of chiral descendants in N=1 superspace; Konishi reproduces the earlier three-loop value, J=1 is new; both equal the predictions of the dilatation operator compatible with BMN scaling.
  Loop order / theory: 3 (planar) / N=4 SYM in N=1 superspace
  Scheme: supersymmetric dimensional reduction (Sec. 3.1), MS-bar (mass-scale conventions in Appendix 6.3); Anselmi's trick (superspace differentiation) lowers the effective loop order by one (Sec. 2.1, eqs. (21)-(23)); ratio of two-point functions of two descendants (eqs. (8), (64)) to avoid normalization and quantum-anomaly problems; contact terms discarded as O(eps) at separated points (Sec. 2.3).
  Symmetry argument: Superconformal multiplet structure (descendants share gamma); protectedness of half-BPS operators used to drop graph sums that are O(eps) (eq. (110), Sec. 3.3.4); a coefficient f_21 fixed by requiring the ratio to be logarithm free (eq. (119)).
  Proved:
    - one-loop gamma_1 = 3N/(4 pi^2) reproduced (eq. (72))
    - three-loop Konishi and J=1 values in SSDR
  Assumed:
    - SSDR preserves the superconformal multiplet structure used in the ratio method
    - half-BPS protection used as input to discard contributions
    - some coefficients fixed by requiring logarithm-free ratios rather than computed
  Checked partially:
    - agreement with BMN-scaling-compatible dilatation operator (Beisert su(2|3), S1a.25)
  Evidence:
    - Sec. 2.1, eqs. (21)-(23): Anselmi's trick
    - eqs. (8), (64): ratio of descendant two-point functions
    - Sec. 3.1: SSDR; Appendix 6.3: MS-bar conventions
    - eq. (72): gamma_1
    - eq. (110): use of half-BPS protection
    - eq. (119): coefficient fixed by absence of logarithms
    - footnote 12: two-loop propagator correction possibly scheme dependent
  Red flags:
    - protected-operator and superconformal-multiplet properties are used as inputs, so this is not an independent test of whether SSDR preserves superconformal symmetry
    - the 'quantum anomaly' of the Konishi multiplet is circumvented by the choice of ratio, i.e. the scheme-dependent anomaly issue (S1a.22) is avoided rather than resolved
