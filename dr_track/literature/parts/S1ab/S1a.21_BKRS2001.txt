[S1a.21_BKRS2001] M. Bianchi, S. Kovacs, G. Rossi, Y. S. Stanev -- Properties of the Konishi multiplet in N=4 SYM theory (2001)
  arXiv: hep-th/0104016   Journal: JHEP 0105 (2001) 042
  Section: 1a N=4 spectrum via Feynman/supergraphs
  Claim: Structure of the long Konishi multiplet of SU(2,2|4); the Konishi anomaly in N=1 superfield form (1/4) Dbar^2 K = tr(Phi^I dW/dPhi^I) + (6 g^2 N/32 pi^2) tr(W^alpha W_alpha) (Sect. 3, eq. (7)) feeds into superdescendants; O(g^2) two- and three-point function checks consistent with gamma_1 = 3N/(4 pi^2) (eq. (21)); gamma_2 = -3N^2/(16 pi^4) (eq. (22)) quoted from OPE analyses; the Delta_0 = 4 20' operator is found unrenormalized at O(g^2), O(g^4).
  Loop order / theory: 1 (explicit), 2 (quoted) / N=4 SYM, Konishi multiplet
  Scheme: operator defined by point splitting with normalization a(g^2) (eq. (17)) and Z ~ (mu^2 eps^2)^{-gamma/2} (eq. (19)); the regularization behind the anomaly equation (7) is not specified in the paper (anomaly coefficient taken from Konishi's original work)
  Symmetry argument: superconformal representation theory (long vs short multiplets, U(1)_Y bonus symmetry); naive SUSY transformations stated to be valid for the component that needs no normal ordering
  Proved:
    - O(g^2) consistency of two- and three-point functions with the stated gamma_1
  Assumed:
    - the anomaly coefficient of eq. (7) (from the literature) and its use with a point-split operator
    - resummation of logarithms to all orders (checked only at O(g^4) for the lowest supercurrent operator, Sect. 5)
  Checked partially:
    - protection of the 20' Delta_0 = 4 operator through O(g^4)
  Evidence:
    - Sect. 3, eq. (7): Konishi anomaly with coefficient 6 g^2 N/(32 pi^2)
    - eq. (17)-(19): point-split definition and multiplicative renormalization
    - eq. (21)-(22): gamma_1, gamma_2
    - Sect. 6: 20' operator at Delta_0 = 4 unrenormalized
  Red flags:
    - the regularization defining the anomaly is not specified; mixing of the anomaly term with the classical term is scheme dependent beyond one loop (see S1a.22)
    - normalization of g differs by a factor 2 from some earlier papers (footnote 3)
  Cross-checks:
    - EJSS hep-th/0501077 (S1a.22) reinterpret the Konishi anomaly as operator mixing in SSDR
  Access / confidence: full text via ar5iv (summarised) / medium
  Notes: A one-loop N=4 composite-operator datum (Konishi anomaly coefficient) whose scheme dependence beyond one loop is exactly the kind of quantity a BV/QME treatment of composite operators should fix.
