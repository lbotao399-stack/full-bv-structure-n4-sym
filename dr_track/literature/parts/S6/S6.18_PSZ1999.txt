[S6.18_PSZ1999] S. Penati, A. Santambrogio, D. Zanon -- Two-point functions of chiral operators in N=4 SYM at order g^4 (1999)
  arXiv: hep-th/9910197   Journal: JHEP 9912 (1999) 006
  Section: 6c all-order protection results with manifestly supersymmetric or algebraic schemes; BPS protection checks in DR
  Claim: The two-point function of the chiral primary Tr Phi^3 in N=4 SU(N) SYM receives no correction through order g^4 for any N; at order g^2 this follows from stress-tensor-multiplet non-renormalization, at g^4 the cancellation is between zeta(3) and zeta(5) pole terms of different diagrams.
  Loop order / theory: 2 (order g^4) / N=4 SU(N) SYM in N=1 superspace
  Scheme: N=1 superspace; dimensional regularization n = 4-2eps for momentum integrals (App. B); minimal subtraction keeping only 1/eps poles; whether the D-algebra is done in 4d is not stated explicitly (Sec. 2 'standard techniques'); uniqueness method for integrals (eqs. B.4-B.10).
  Symmetry argument: Conformal invariance fixes the x-dependence (eq. 2.4); vanishing shown for the 1/eps poles only; finite momentum-space parts claimed to be contact terms (Secs. 2, 5).
  Proved:
      - Cancellation of all 1/eps poles at O(g^4): 6a -(3/2)zeta3, 6b +3zeta3, 6i -5zeta5, 6j +5zeta5-(3/2)zeta3 (eqs. 4.6, 4.8, 4.9, 4.21); non-planar colour factors vanish (App. A).
  Assumed:
      - The regularization preserves the relevant (super)symmetry; not discussed.
      - Finite parts irrelevant (contact terms).
  Checked partially:
      - Only one operator (Tr Phi^3) and only pole terms.
  Evidence:
      - Sec. 2 eq. (2.4); Sec. 4 eqs. (4.3)-(4.21); App. A eqs. (A.18)-(A.21); App. B eqs. (B.4)-(B.10).
  Red flags:
      - Protection is checked on 1/eps poles only; finite scheme-dependent terms are not tested.
      - N=4 SUSY only non-linearly realized in N=1 superspace.
  Cross-checks:
      - S1a.07_Sieg2010 (Sec. 4 links BPS protection and conformal invariance)
  Access / confidence: full text via ar5iv / high
  Notes: Representative two-loop DR check of BPS protection for a composite operator.
